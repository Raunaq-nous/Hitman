    Cricket

    Three wickets in overs 16 to 18 flipped the chase: the defending side went from a 28% to a 71% chance of winning.

    The required rate climbed past 14 an over and the set batter was gone, so the last two overs were a scramble.

    F1

    An early stop on lap 22 gained 3.1 seconds on the leader, and the lead never changed hands again.

    The out-lap on new tyres was 2.4 seconds quicker than the leader’s in-lap on old ones, and the stop itself was 0.7 seconds faster.

    Source: lap and sector timing for laps 20 to 26, plus pit-lane times for both cars.

    Football

    A pressing trap in the 61st minute won the ball 30 metres from goal. The winner came 14 seconds later.

    The press was triggered by a backward pass to the left centre-back. Three players closed the passing lanes and the ball was won in the left half-space.

    Source: on-ball events from the 60th to the 62nd minute, with the positions of the pressing players at the moment of the turnover.

    Two break points saved at 3–3 in the deciding set, and the break came in the very next game.

    Both break points were saved with first serves, and the returner didn’t win a first-serve return in that game.

    The roster

    Five specialist analysts, each built around one legend’s way of reading the game. They don’t pretend to be the legend. They think like the mentality that made the name.

    The Opener

    Cricket

    Mentality
    Timing over force. Turn a start into something enormous.
    Signature tool
    Over Call: call the next over before it’s bowled, then see how the numbers read it.
    Lineage
    Rohit Sharma, “Hitman”. Bengaluru, 2 November 2013: 209 off 158 balls with 16 sixes. A broadcast crew member called him the Hitman that day, and the commentary box made it stick.

    The Closer

    Basketball

    Mentality
    Study the film until nothing surprises you, then want the last shot.
    Signature tool
    Film Room: every possession broken down, and a daily drill that keeps your streak alive.
    Lineage
    Kobe Bryant, “Black Mamba”. Los Angeles, 22 January 2006: 81 points against Toronto in a single game.

    The Architect

    Football

    Mentality
    See the pass before it exists.
    Signature tool
    Blueprint: the shape of the game drawn as it happens, with what-if set pieces.
    Lineage
    Andrea Pirlo, “L’Architetto”. Dortmund, 4 July 2006: the no-look pass in the 119th minute that sent Italy to the World Cup final.

    The Professor

    F1

    Mentality
    Take only the risk the race demands.
    Signature tool
    Pit Wall: make the strategy call, then watch the simulation race it out.
    Lineage
    Alain Prost, “The Professor”. Adelaide, 1986: won the race and the championship by two points after his main rival’s tyre exploded.

    The Iceman

    Tennis

    Mentality
    Nothing changes on the big points.
    Signature tool
    Pressure Index: who holds when the point matters most.
    Lineage
    Björn Borg, “Ice-Borg”. Wimbledon, 5 July 1980: lost a fourth-set tiebreak 16–18, then won the fifth 8–6 for a fifth straight title.

    Next on the bench: golf, American football, cycling and baseball.
